# Visual language and interaction design

The product combines a blocky green enclosure, black face areas, a pixel plant and warm paper. Its story is a workday companion, so the interface prioritizes one glance and one useful action over an industrial dashboard full of controls.

## Screen hierarchy

The face/companion page leads into today's state, an information page, a photo page and a focus-timer page. Settings provides language, themes, brightness, Wi-Fi, planter connection, calibration and light thresholds. The screen supports Chinese and English; translating this website does not change a connected device's language setting.

Species, growth, game health and mood remain separate from live sensor fields. Invalid values should read as unavailable or uncalibrated, not as a plausible zero. Connection feedback distinguishes network, controller, phone binding and request processing.

## Themes and motion

Themes change palette and character atmosphere without hiding actions or changing units. Inspiration can come from animation, landscapes or mechanical/cyber visuals; reproduction assets must respect the rights of their creators. Use original motifs rather than implying affiliation with a named franchise or studio.

Motion should express a state: blinking, leaves, a focus pulse or a printing progression. It should not shake text, mask a stale value or require motion to understand a status. The Indicator's stable double-buffered display is a hardware result; a browser mockup does not establish firmware timing.

The English landing page has a labelled browser-only interaction preview, keyboard-accessible controls and reduced-motion support. It does not connect to the physical planter. Printed tickets use strong monochrome contrast, generous handwriting space and the tested raster mode. See [ticket design](daily-ticket.md).
